Sustainability Consulting

Discover what sustainability consulting involves: how consultants help organisations become more sustainable, from strategy to reporting, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an application.

View vacanciesExplore consulting paths

Introduction

What is sustainability consulting?

Sustainability consulting is about how an organisation becomes more sustainable: less emissions, a circular way of operating, and meeting the growing flow of sustainability rules. Consultants help companies set a sustainability strategy and actually carry it out.

The work combines strategy, figures and regulation: a sustainability plan is only credible if it is measurable and fits the business strategy. The consultant therefore switches between ambition, data and what is practically feasible.

Sustainability is one of the fastest-growing parts of consulting, partly due to rules such as the CSRD. It appeals to students who want to combine societal impact with work at the top of organisations.

What it isThe advisory field around making organisations more sustainable, from strategy to reporting.
What it centres onTurning sustainability ambitions into a measurable and feasible plan.
Why clients need itTo become more sustainable, meet new rules and report credibly.
Why students find it attractiveCombining societal impact with strategic work and a growing field.

Step by step

How a sustainability process unfolds

Most sustainability projects follow a similar path, from a baseline to measuring and reporting progress. That cycle repeats, because goals get sharpened and rules change.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior consultants do much of the measuring and analysis, while senior consultants set the ambition and bring the client along toward the board.

The value of a sustainability process often sits in the last steps: an ambition that is not measured and reported stays non-committal.

Roles

Roles in the sustainability practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from analysis toward leading teams and managing the client relationship. Most consultants climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving to a sustainability role in the business is common at any level.

The entry role straight after a bachelor's or master's.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

Sustainability consulting has a relatively fast career path, partly due to the strong growth of the field. The pace differs by firm and individual.

Many consultants stay a few years and then move to a sustainability role in the business or at a non-profit. Those who stay progress toward Manager and eventually partner or director.

Because the field is young and growing fast, consultants who join early can progress relatively quickly.

LevelTypical time in role
Business Analyst2 to 3 years
Consultant / Associate2 to 4 years
Engagement Manager2 to 4 years
Partnertop level, 10+ years in total

Compensation

Compensation in sustainability consulting

Compensation usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at many firms a bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Pay depends strongly on the type of employer: at a strategy house it is typically higher than at a small specialist firm.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior consultant

The day often starts with analysing the client's emissions or sustainability data. After that, much of the time goes into working out a roadmap or preparing a report according to the applicable rules.

A sustainability consultant switches between ambition and feasibility: a plan has to be both credible and practical. That requires numerical work and the ability to bring the organisation along.

On a day with a client discussion, the pace shifts: the team shows the progress, discusses the choices with the client and advises on the next steps.

Juniors

What junior consultants do

Junior consultants carry much of the measuring and analysis work a sustainability project rests on, under the supervision of a Manager.

  • Analysing emissions and data
  • Helping build roadmaps
  • Researching regulation
  • Preparing reports
  • Building benchmarks
  • Tracking progress
  • Joining client discussions
  • Aligning with the team

Much of this work goes back and forth within the team and with the client before a plan or report is ready. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior consultant learns what makes a sustainability plan credible and feasible.

Compare

Sustainability versus other paths

These paths sometimes overlap, which is why students mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in the focus on sustainability.

Sustainability vs Strategy Consulting

Strategy decides an organisation's broader choices. Sustainability focuses specifically on becoming more sustainable and the goals around it.

Sustainability vs Risk & Regulatory

Risk & regulatory manages risk and rules broadly. Sustainability focuses on sustainability and reporting such as the CSRD.

Sustainability vs Operations Consulting

Operations improves execution in general. Sustainability does so with sustainability as the goal, for example in the supply chain.

Sustainability consulting vs ESG in law

The legal side of ESG and sustainability rules belongs to the legal path. Sustainability consulting focuses on strategy, execution and reporting.

Sustainability consulting vs an in-house sustainability role

An in-house role works for a single organisation. As a consultant you work for many clients and see many different approaches in a short time.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is sustainability consulting?

It is the advisory field around making organisations more sustainable: consultants help set a sustainability strategy, reduce emissions, work circularly and meet rules such as the CSRD.

What degree do I need?

The routes are broad. Students come from business, economics, technical and sustainability programmes. Affinity with sustainability and analytical ability are important.

What does a sustainability consultant do day to day?

Consultants set strategy, measure emissions, build roadmaps, guide CSRD reporting and help organisations meet their sustainability goals.

What is the CSRD?

The CSRD is the European directive that requires large companies to report on their sustainability, which has strongly increased demand for sustainability consulting.

Is sustainability consulting a growing field?

Yes. Partly due to rules such as the CSRD and the attention to climate, sustainability is one of the fastest-growing parts of consulting.

How much do you earn in sustainability consulting?

Pay depends strongly on the type of employer and is typically higher at a strategy house than at a small specialist firm. Actual pay is listed where known in the vacancies on this site.

What skills do you need?

Analytical thinking, knowledge of sustainability, numeracy, communication and the ability to connect ambition and feasibility.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Get to know sustainability and the CSRD, do a relevant internship, and apply via campus or the firm's website.
