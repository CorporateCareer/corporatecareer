Financial & Deal Advisory

Discover what financial & deal advisory involves: how advisers guide companies and investors through acquisitions, valuations and restructurings, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an application.

Introduction

What is financial & deal advisory?

Financial & deal advisory is about the financial side of transactions and special situations: acquisitions, valuations, financial due diligence and restructurings. Advisers guide companies and investors through decisions where a lot of money and risk is at stake.

The work is analytical and numerical: the adviser assesses the figures behind a deal, maps the risks, and substantiates what a company is worth. Precision and speed under time pressure are crucial.

This field sits close to the world of corporate finance and investment banking, but from the advisory role. It appeals to students with a strong financial interest who want to work on transactions.

What it isThe advisory field around the financial side of acquisitions, valuations and restructurings.
What it centres onAssessing and substantiating the figures and risks behind a transaction.
Why clients need itTo buy, sell, value or restructure with confidence.
Why students find it attractiveNumerical work on transactions, close to the world of dealmaking.

Step by step

How deal advisory unfolds in a transaction

Most deals follow a similar path, from preparation to closing and sometimes integration. The adviser is involved at different steps, depending on their role.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior advisers do much of the analysis and modelling, while senior advisers interpret the findings and support the client at the negotiating table.

Not every deal reaches a closing: due diligence can surface risks that make a buyer walk away or adjust the price.

Roles

Roles in the deal advisory practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from analysis toward leading teams and managing the client relationship. Most advisers climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving to corporate finance, private equity or the business is common at any level.

Analyst / Associate

The entry role straight after a bachelor's or master's.

Key tasks
Analysing figures, building models, preparing reports.
Core skills
Numeracy, precision, financial knowledge.
Typical level
Entry-level, after studying.

Consultant / Senior Associate

Key tasks
Running due diligence or valuation, client contact, guiding juniors.
Core skills
Analytical ability, financial insight, communication.
Typical level

Manager

Leads a team and the day-to-day running of a deal project.

Key tasks
Steering the project, managing the team, dealing with the client daily.
Core skills
Leadership, oversight, client management.

Senior Manager / Director

Carries responsibility for several deals and client relationships.

Key tasks
Building client relationships, ensuring quality, winning new engagements.
Core skills
Commercial judgement, sector knowledge, network.
Typical level
Senior, on the way to partner.

Partner

Holds ultimate responsibility for clients and the practice.

Key tasks
Client relationships, business development, ultimate responsibility for the firm.
Core skills

Typical level

Career timeline

How long each step takes

Financial & deal advisory has a relatively fast career path, comparable to other parts of the advisory world. The pace differs by firm and individual.

Many advisers stay a few years and then move into corporate finance, private equity or a financial role in the business. Those who stay progress toward Manager and eventually partner.

An additional qualification such as RA, RC or CFA is common in this field and can support progression.

LevelTypical time in role
Analyst / Associate2 to 3 years
Consultant2 to 4 years
Manager2 to 4 years
Partnertop level, 10+ years in total

Compensation usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at many firms a bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Deal advisory pays well and sits, in level, between consulting and the corporate-finance world.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior adviser

The day often starts with going through the figures and documents of an acquisition target. After that, much of the time goes into analysing the financial data, building models and drafting findings for the report.

A deal adviser works in short, intensive engagements with tight deadlines, because a transaction has a fixed timetable. That requires precise work under pressure and a lot of alignment with the client and the other advisers.

On a day heading toward closing, the pace shifts: the team finishes the last findings, discusses the risks with the client, and supports the negotiation over price and terms.

Juniors

What junior advisers do

Junior advisers carry much of the analysis and modelling work a deal rests on, under the supervision of a Manager.

  • Analysing financial data
  • Building models
  • Looking for trends and anomalies
  • Working through valuations
  • Documenting findings
  • Going through the data room
  • Preparing reports
  • Aligning with other advisers

Much of this work goes back and forth within the team before a finding lands in the report. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior adviser learns which figures in a deal really matter.

Compare

Financial & Deal Advisory versus other paths

These paths sometimes overlap, which is why students mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in the role and the part of the deal.

Financial & Deal Advisory vs Strategy Consulting

Strategy assesses the commercial attractiveness of a deal. Deal advisory assesses the financial figures and risks underneath it.

Financial & Deal Advisory vs Investment Banking

Investment banking guides the transaction and the financing. Deal advisory provides the financial analysis and due diligence around it.

Financial & Deal Advisory vs Transaction Services (finance)

Transaction services is the finance path that describes financial due diligence. This consulting path takes a broader view of the advisory and deal side.

Financial & Deal Advisory vs Restructuring & Insolvency (legal)

The legal side of restructuring belongs to the legal path. Deal advisory does the financial side of the turnaround.

Financial & Deal Advisory vs Operations Consulting

Operations improves a company's execution. Deal advisory assesses and improves the financial position, often around a transaction.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is financial & deal advisory?

It is the advisory field around the financial side of transactions and special situations: acquisitions, valuations, financial due diligence and restructurings.

What degree do I need?

Most advisers come from a financial or economics degree such as accountancy, finance, business economics or econometrics. Strong numerical skills are important.

What does a deal adviser do day to day?

Advisers run financial due diligence, prepare valuations, build models, guide acquisitions and work on restructurings.

What is the difference with investment banking?

Investment banking guides the transaction and the financing, while deal advisory provides the financial analysis and due diligence around it. The worlds sit close together.

What is financial due diligence?

Financial due diligence is scrutinising the figures of an acquisition target to map risks and opportunities for a buyer or seller.

How much do you earn in deal advisory?

Deal advisory pays well and sits, in level, between consulting and the corporate-finance world. Actual pay varies by firm and is listed where known in the vacancies on this site.

What skills do you need?

Numeracy, financial knowledge, analytical thinking, financial modelling and the ability to explain a financial finding clearly.
