Consulting career path

Operations Consulting

Discover what operations consulting involves: how consultants help organisations improve their processes, supply chain and execution, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an application.

View vacanciesExplore consulting paths

Introduction

What is operations consulting?

Operations consulting is about an organisation's execution: how products are made, how the supply chain runs and how processes can be more efficient and reliable. Where strategy consulting decides where an organisation should go, operations focuses on how the work gets done better.

The work is concrete and measurable: the consultant measures how processes run now, finds the bottlenecks, and designs improvements that deliver demonstrable results in practice, in cost, speed or quality.

Operations is one of the oldest and most tangible parts of consulting. It appeals to students who like concrete improvement and enjoy working close to the shop floor.

What it isThe advisory field around improving processes, supply chain and execution.
What it centres onMaking processes more efficient, faster and more reliable with demonstrable results.
Why clients need itTo lower costs, raise quality and deliver more reliably.
Why students find it attractiveConcrete, measurable work close to the shop floor, with tangible results.

What they do

What operations consultants do

The work differs by project, but the building blocks below come up in almost every operations project.

Measuring and mapping processesWorking out how a process really runs now and where it gets stuck.
Analysing bottlenecksFinding the causes of delay, waste or errors.
Redesigning processesDesigning a better way of working that is more efficient and reliable.
Optimising the supply chainMaking the chain from procurement to delivery smarter and more robust.
Reducing costsStructurally saving costs without harming quality.
Lean and continuous improvementHelping teams make and sustain improvements themselves.
Guiding changeBringing the shop floor along so a new way of working sticks.
Embedding resultsMaking sure the improvement keeps working after the project.

Step by step

How an operations project unfolds

Most operations projects follow a similar path, from measuring the current situation to embedding the improvement. Many firms use a structured approach such as Lean.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior consultants do much of the measuring and analysis on the shop floor, while senior consultants set the approach and lead the change with the client.

The value of an operations project often sits in the last step: an improvement that is not embedded fades away over time.

Roles

Roles in the operations practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from analysis on the shop floor toward leading teams and managing the client relationship. Most consultants climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving to an operations role in the business is common at any level.

The entry role straight after a bachelor's or master's.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

Operations consulting has a relatively fast career path. The pace differs by firm and individual, and relates to the projects and results you show.

Many consultants stay a few years and then move to an operations role in the business, for example in production, supply chain or procurement. Those who stay progress toward Manager and eventually partner or director.

A certification such as Lean Six Sigma is common in this field and can support progression.

LevelTypical time in role
Consultant2 to 3 years
Senior Consultant2 to 4 years
Manager2 to 4 years
Partnertop level, 10+ years in total

Compensation

Compensation in operations consulting

Compensation usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at many firms a bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Operations consulting pays well, at a level comparable to other parts of consulting.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior consultant

The day often starts on site at the client, on the shop floor or in the factory. Much of the time goes into measuring processes, talking to staff and analysing the data that comes out of it.

An operations consultant works close to execution: the improvement has to work not only on paper but also on the shop floor. That requires practical insight and the ability to bring people along.

On a day with a steering committee, the pace shifts: the team summarises the findings, shows the results to the client's leadership and discusses the next steps.

Juniors

What junior consultants do

Junior consultants carry much of the measuring and analysis work an operations project rests on, under the supervision of a Manager.

  • Measuring processes on the shop floor
  • Gathering and analysing data
  • Mapping bottlenecks
  • Helping design improvements
  • Talking to staff
  • Working through the results
  • Tracking progress
  • Supporting the implementation

Much of this work goes back and forth between the shop floor and the team before an improvement is proposed. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior consultant learns what really works in practice.

Compare

Operations versus other paths

These paths sometimes overlap, which is why students mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in the type of work and how close you sit to execution.

Operations vs Strategy Consulting

Strategy decides where an organisation should go. Operations focuses on how the work gets done better and more efficiently.

Operations vs Technology & Digital Consulting

Technology & digital improves with technology as the engine. Operations improves the processes themselves, sometimes with and sometimes without new technology.

Operations vs Data & Analytics

Data & analytics builds models and insights. Operations uses those insights to concretely improve execution.

Operations consulting vs an in-house operations role

An in-house role works for a single organisation. As a consultant you work for many clients and see many different processes in a short time.

Operations vs Financial & Deal Advisory

Deal advisory assesses the financial side of a company. Operations improves execution, sometimes as part of an improvement programme after a deal.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is operations consulting?

It is the advisory field around execution: consultants help organisations make their processes, supply chain and way of working more efficient and reliable.

What degree do I need?

The routes are broad. Students come from business, economics, technical and logistics programmes. Affinity with processes and figures matters more than the exact field.

What does an operations consultant do day to day?

Consultants measure processes, analyse bottlenecks, design improvements, guide implementation and embed the result, often close to the shop floor.

What is the difference with strategy consulting?

Strategy decides where an organisation should go, while operations focuses on how the work gets done better and more efficiently.

What is Lean?

Lean is a structured way to improve processes by reducing waste and enabling teams to continuously improve themselves.

How much do you earn in operations consulting?

Operations consulting pays well, at a level comparable to other parts of consulting. Actual pay varies by firm and is listed where known in the vacancies on this site.

What skills do you need?

Analytical thinking, numeracy, a practical mindset, communication and the ability to bring people on the shop floor along.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Learn the basics of process improvement, do a relevant internship, practise cases, and apply via campus or the firm's website.
