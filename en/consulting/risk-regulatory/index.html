Risk & Regulatory Consulting

Discover what risk & regulatory consulting involves: how consultants help organisations manage risk and comply with regulation, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an application.

View vacanciesExplore consulting paths

Introduction

What is risk & regulatory consulting?

Risk & regulatory consulting is about how an organisation manages its risks and complies with the rules. Consultants help map risks, set up controls, and meet a growing flow of regulation, from financial to operational and digital risks.

The work is structured and detailed: a good risk framework is only valuable if it matches reality and can be tested by a regulator. The consultant therefore combines analysis with knowledge of rules and processes.

Risk & regulatory is large in the Netherlands, partly due to the strong financial sector and the many regulators. It appeals to students who like structure and rules and want to work close to decision-making.

What it isThe advisory field around managing risk and complying with regulation.
What it centres onMapping risks, managing them and demonstrably complying with the rules.
Why clients need itTo avoid fines and incidents and keep the trust of regulators.
Why students find it attractiveStructured work with societal relevance, close to decision-making.

Broader advisory firms often approach risk from a larger transformation. In addition, many risk specialists work in-house at a bank, insurer or regulator.

What they do

What risk & regulatory consultants do

The work differs by engagement, but the building blocks below come up in almost every risk project.

Mapping risksWorking out which risks an organisation runs and how large they are.
Setting up controlsDesigning controls and frameworks that make risks manageable.
Building compliance programmesMaking sure an organisation meets the rules that apply to it.
Translating regulationTranslating new laws and regulation into what an organisation must do.
Internal auditTesting whether the controls work in practice.
Validating modelsAssessing whether risk models are reliable and defensible.
Dealing with regulatorsAssisting clients in their contact with the regulator.
Reporting and accountabilityClearly reporting the risk position and compliance to the board and regulators.

Step by step

How a risk process unfolds

Most risk projects follow a similar cycle, from identifying risks to reporting on them. That cycle repeats, because risks and rules change constantly.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior consultants do much of the analysis and testing, while senior consultants set the approach and support the client toward the board and regulator.

The value of a risk process often sits in the last steps: only controls that are monitored and accounted for offer real assurance.

Roles

Roles in the risk practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from analysis toward leading teams and managing the client relationship. Most consultants climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving to a risk or compliance role in the business is common at any level.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

Risk & regulatory consulting has a relatively structured career path. The pace differs by firm and individual, and relates to the engagements and results you show.

Many consultants stay a few years and then move to a risk, compliance or audit role at a bank, insurer or regulator. Those who stay progress toward Manager and eventually partner.

An additional qualification such as RA, RE or a risk or compliance certification is common in this field and can support progression.

LevelTypical time in role
Consultant2 to 3 years
Senior Consultant2 to 4 years
Manager2 to 4 years
Partnertop level, 10+ years in total

Compensation usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at many firms a bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Risk & regulatory consulting pays well, at a level comparable to other parts of the advisory world.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior consultant

The day often starts with going through the client's documents and data. After that, much of the time goes into analysing risks, testing controls and preparing findings for a report.

A risk consultant works in a structured and precise way, because a finding has to be testable for the client and sometimes for a regulator. That requires knowledge of rules and attention to detail.

On a day with a client discussion, the pace shifts: the team summarises the findings, discusses the risks with the client and advises on the next steps.

Juniors

What junior consultants do

Junior consultants carry much of the analysis and testing work a risk engagement rests on, under the supervision of a Manager.

  • Analysing risks and documents
  • Testing controls
  • Researching regulation
  • Gathering and checking data
  • Documenting findings
  • Preparing reports
  • Joining client discussions
  • Aligning with the team

Much of this work goes back and forth within the team before a finding lands in the report. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior consultant learns which risks really matter.

Compare

Risk & Regulatory versus other paths

These paths sometimes overlap, which is why students mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in the focus on risk and rules.

Risk & Regulatory vs Strategy Consulting

Strategy decides an organisation's choices. Risk & regulatory makes sure those choices stay within the rules and the risks are managed.

Risk & Regulatory vs Technology & Digital Consulting

Technology & digital builds solutions. Risk & regulatory assesses the risks of them, including cyber and technology risk.

Risk & Regulatory vs Financial & Deal Advisory

Deal advisory assesses the figures around a transaction. Risk & regulatory focuses on the ongoing management of risk and compliance.

Risk & Regulatory vs legal compliance (legal)

The legal side of regulation belongs to the legal path. Risk & regulatory translates the rules into processes and controls.

Risk consulting vs an in-house risk role

An in-house role works for a single organisation. As a consultant you work for many clients and see many different risks in a short time.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is risk & regulatory consulting?

It is the advisory field around managing risk and complying with regulation: consultants help map risks, set up controls and meet the rules.

What degree do I need?

Most consultants come from a degree in finance, economics, accountancy, law or business. Affinity with rules, risk and figures is important.

What does a risk consultant do day to day?

Consultants map risks, set up controls, build compliance programmes, test via internal audit and report to the board and regulators.

Which sector do you mainly work in?

Risk & regulatory is large in the financial sector, with much work for banks, insurers and regulators, but it also occurs in other sectors.

What is the difference with legal compliance?

The legal side of regulation belongs to the legal path, while risk & regulatory translates the rules into processes, controls and reporting.

How much do you earn in risk consulting?

Risk consulting pays well, at a level comparable to other parts of the advisory world. Actual pay varies by firm and is listed where known in the vacancies on this site.

What skills do you need?

Analytical thinking, attention to detail, knowledge of rules, structured working and the ability to explain a complex rule clearly.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Get to know the financial sector and risk management, do a relevant internship, and apply via campus or the firm's website.
