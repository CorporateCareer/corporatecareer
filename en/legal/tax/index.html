Tax

Discover what tax, known in the Netherlands as fiscaal recht or belastingrecht, means in legal practice: how tax advisers and lawyers advise companies on taxation, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

Introduction

What is tax (fiscaal recht)?

Tax, known in the Netherlands as fiscaal recht or belastingrecht, centres on how much tax a company or individual owes and how that obligation can be arranged as efficiently as possible within the rules. It combines legislation, case law and figures in advice where large amounts are often at stake.

Within tax there are two closely related tracks. The tax lawyer works at a law firm and focuses on tax structuring and disputes, while the tax adviser or fiscalist often works at one of the Big Four or a specialist firm. In tax disputes the Dutch Tax Administration is the counterparty, with its own procedural law based on the General Tax Act.

Tax is not a standalone field, it runs through other practice areas. An acquisition, a real estate transaction, a private equity fund or a restructuring all have tax consequences that often determine how the transaction is ultimately shaped.

What it isThe practice area covering taxation and the tax position of companies and individuals.
What it centres onArranging transactions and structures correctly and efficiently within the limits of the law.
Why clients need itTo pay tax correctly, avoid double taxation and manage tax risk.
Why students find it attractiveIntellectually sharp questions, international matters and figures with direct impact.

The practice

Three settings where you practise this field

The breakdown below mainly says something about the type of employer and the client you serve, not about the quality of the work.

Tax and law firmFirms where tax advisers and lawyers work together and tax is a core practice, not a sideline.
Full-service law firm with a tax sectionA tax practice alongside corporate/M&A and finance, often brought in for transactions.
Big Four or specialist tax advisory firmLarge accountancy and advisory firms with extensive tax advisory practices.

At tax and law firms such as Loyens & Loeff, tax advisers and lawyers work closely together. At full-service firms, tax sits close to corporate/M&A and finance, because almost every transaction has tax consequences. At the Big Four and specialist tax advisory firms you usually do not follow the lawyer traineeship, but the tax adviser track, with its own professional training and qualification. In addition, many tax specialists work in-house in the tax department of a multinational.

What they do

What tax advisers do

The work differs by matter, but the building blocks below come up in almost every tax matter.

Setting up tax structuresArranging a transaction or company so that the tax consequences are correct and efficient.
Tax due diligenceMapping the tax risks of an acquisition target before a deal is signed.
Advising on corporate income taxAdvising companies on their corporate income tax position, participation exemption and loss relief.
Handling VAT questionsAssessing the VAT consequences of supplies, services and cross-border trade.
Documenting transfer pricingSubstantiating that intra-group transfer prices are at arm's length, as the law and the OECD require.
Conducting tax disputesLitigating against a tax assessment, from objection to cassation before the Supreme Court.
Applying for rulingsSeeking advance certainty from the Tax Administration on the tax treatment of a structure.
Coordinating with other practice groupsWorking with corporate/M&A, finance and restructuring when a matter has tax aspects.

Legal framework

The key legislation

Tax revolves around a set of core statutes that keep recurring, supplemented by European directives and tax treaties that shape the international dimension.

Corporate Income Tax Act 1969Governs the tax on company profits, with the participation exemption as a key concept.
Turnover Tax Act 1968Governs VAT on supplies and services, closely intertwined with the European VAT system.
Income Tax Act 2001Governs the tax on individuals' income, split across three boxes.
General Tax Act (AWR)Contains the general rules on assessments, objection, appeal and the powers of the Tax Administration.
Dividend Withholding Tax Act 1965Governs the levy on dividend distributions, relevant in international structures.
EU directives and tax treatiesFrom ATAD and the Parent-Subsidiary Directive to the OECD Model Convention, decisive in cross-border situations.

Practice areas

The main sub-areas

Tax is broad, and most advisers specialise within one of the sub-areas below.

Indirect taxesVAT, customs duties and excise, strongly shaped by European law.

Step by step

How a tax procedure unfolds

Most tax disputes follow a similar path, from the assessment by the Tax Administration to a final ruling by the highest court. Not every case runs through all the steps; many disputes already end at the objection stage.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior tax specialists work out the facts and figures and draft the objection and appeal, while senior advisers argue the hearing and set the strategy for the case.

A tax procedure can take years if it ultimately reaches cassation before the Supreme Court by way of the district court and the court of appeal. Supreme Court rulings often have consequences for a far broader group of taxpayers than just the party in the case.

Roles

Roles in the tax practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from working out the file toward setting tax strategy and managing the client relationship. Most advisers climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving between firms or between law firms and the Big Four is common at any level.

Supports tax advisers and lawyers during an internship alongside or after studies.

The start of the career, either as a trainee lawyer or as a junior tax adviser.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The pace of progression differs by track. Those who start as a tax lawyer complete the statutory lawyer traineeship, which in principle lasts three years. Those who start as a tax adviser at the Big Four or a specialist firm follow their own professional training, for example toward membership of a professional body for tax advisers.

After the first years, the emphasis shifts from working out files to advising independently and leading tax processes. Making partner takes more than ten years of experience at most firms.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Moving between law firms, the Big Four and an in-house tax role at a company is common, with advisers bringing their experience along.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior tax specialist

The day often starts with working out a tax question for a client or assessing the tax position in a live transaction. On days without a deadline, much of the time goes into drafting advice memos and researching legislation and case law.

A tax specialist often works closely with the corporate or finance teams: as soon as a transaction takes shape, the tax structure has to be right. That requires moving quickly between the client, the other advisers and sometimes the Tax Administration.

On a day with a tax procedure, the pace shifts. The adviser prepares the objection or appeal, anticipates the Tax Administration's positions, and supports the client during the hearing before the tax chamber.

Juniors

What junior tax specialists do

Junior tax specialists carry much of the research and calculation work a tax opinion rests on, under the supervision of a senior.

  • Researching tax legislation and case law
  • Drafting advice memos
  • Calculating tax positions
  • Due diligence of the data room
  • Helping draft objections and appeals
  • Tracking Supreme Court case law
  • Sitting in on hearings and client meetings
  • Aligning with corporate and finance teams

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between junior and senior before advice goes out. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior tax specialist learns which tax risks in a matter really matter.

Compare

Tax versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in which part of the matter is at the centre.

Tax vs Corporate/M&A

Corporate/M&A structures the transaction between parties. Tax structures the tax consequences of that same transaction.

Tax vs Administrative Law

Administrative Law conducts general administrative law. Tax has its own procedural law with the Tax Administration as a fixed counterparty.

Tax vs Banking & Finance

Banking & Finance structures the financing itself. Tax determines the tax treatment of that same financing.

Tax vs Restructuring & Insolvency

Restructuring & Insolvency guides the restructuring process. Tax structures the tax consequences of that restructuring.

Tax vs Dispute Resolution

Dispute Resolution conducts the broader civil procedural law. Tax applies procedural law specifically to disputes with the Tax Administration.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is tax or fiscaal recht?

Tax, known in the Netherlands as fiscaal recht or belastingrecht, centres on the tax a company or individual owes and how that obligation can be arranged efficiently within the rules.

What is the difference between a tax adviser and a tax lawyer?

A tax lawyer works at a law firm and completes the lawyer traineeship, with an emphasis on structuring and disputes. A tax adviser or fiscalist often works at the Big Four or a specialist firm and follows their own professional training. In practice the two tracks overlap heavily.

What does a tax adviser do day to day?

Tax advisers set up tax structures, carry out tax due diligence, advise on corporate income tax and VAT, document transfer pricing, and conduct tax disputes against the Tax Administration.

Do you need a specific master's degree for tax?

A master's in tax law or tax economics is the most direct route. Both connect to practice, with tax law being more legal and tax economics more numerical.

Which courts handle tax cases in the Netherlands?

After the objection stage, tax cases start at the tax chamber of a district court, go on higher appeal to a court of appeal, and can reach the Supreme Court in cassation.

What is the role of the Tax Administration in a tax dispute?

The Tax Administration issues the assessment and is the fixed counterparty in a tax dispute. The procedure follows the General Tax Act, starting with an objection stage at the administration itself.

What skills do you need for tax?

Analytical thinking, precise writing, numeracy, attention to detail, and the ability to make a complex tax outcome understandable to a client.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take a master's in tax law or tax economics, do an internship at a firm or at the Big Four, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation through several tax sub-areas.
