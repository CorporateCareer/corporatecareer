Legal career path

Commercial

Discover what commercial (contract law) means in legal practice: how lawyers draft the contracts that let businesses do business with each other, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

Introduction

What is commercial (contract law)?

Commercial, often called contract law or trade law in the Netherlands, covers the agreements businesses use to deal with one another: purchase and sale contracts, distribution and agency agreements, licences and cooperation arrangements. It is the practice area that puts the day-to-day trading relationships between companies into legally binding form.

Unlike a one-off acquisition, commercial work is often about ongoing relationships: a supplier delivering to the same buyer for years, a distributor representing a brand in a country, or a licensee allowed to use another company's technology. The lawyer's job is to make the arrangement clear before any disagreement arises.

Commercial lawyers often work closely with other practice groups: a webshop's terms and conditions touch on consumer law, a licence agreement touches on intellectual property, and an international supply agreement raises questions of applicable law and dispute resolution.

What it isThe practice area covering the contracts businesses use to deal with each other.
What it centres onWho supplies what, on what terms, and what happens if a party fails to honour the agreement.
Why clients need itTo put trading relationships, distribution and licensing on terms that hold up.
Why students find it attractiveBroad exposure across different sectors, plenty of client contact, and direct, practical questions.

The practice

Three settings where you practise this field

The breakdown below mainly says something about the client you serve, not about the quality of the work. The same contract question can come up at an international firm, a Dutch full-service firm, or in-house at a company, each with a slightly different angle.

International firmFirms that handle cross-border trade contracts and international distribution networks.
Dutch full-service firmA commercial practice alongside other practice groups, often close to IP or corporate.
In-house at a companyCompany lawyers who review and draft the organisation's own purchase, sale and cooperation contracts.

At international firms you often work on cross-border supply and distribution agreements with parties in multiple countries. At Dutch full-service firms, the commercial practice often sits directly alongside the intellectual property or corporate team. In-house at a company, you review contracts from the perspective of your own organisation, with a lot of repetition and volume.

What they do

What commercial lawyers do

The work differs by sector, but the building blocks below come up in almost every commercial matter. A lawyer often works on several contracts at once, from a first draft to the final signature.

Drafting contractsDrafting purchase, sale and cooperation agreements tailored to the client.
Drafting terms and conditionsWriting standard terms and testing them against the statutory rules that govern them.
Distribution and agency agreementsSetting out the arrangement between a supplier and its distributors or commercial agents.
Drafting licence agreementsRecording the terms under which a party may use another's technology or brand.
Negotiating with the counterpartyNegotiating price, liability and delivery terms on the client's behalf.
Reviewing contract portfoliosAssessing existing contracts during an acquisition or an internal review.
Preventing disputesFlagging risks before a breach of contract turns into litigation.
Coordinating with other practice groupsWorking with IP or competition teams when a contract touches on those areas.

Legal framework

The key legislation

Commercial revolves around a limited set of core rules from the law of obligations that keep recurring, even though the substance differs greatly from contract to contract.

Books 6 and 7 of the Dutch Civil CodeThe general rules on obligations and the specific contracts such as sale, mandate and agency.
Vienna Convention (CISG)The treaty governing international sale contracts between businesses, unless the parties exclude it.
Commercial agency rulesThe EU directive and Dutch rules that protect the position of a commercial agent, including on termination.
Unfair Commercial Practices DirectiveRules that limit unfair terms in the relationship between businesses and consumers.
Terms and conditions rules (Articles 6:231-247 Dutch Civil Code)The rules on when terms and conditions apply and when a clause can be voided.
Rome I RegulationThe EU rules that determine which law applies to an international agreement.

Step by step

How a commercial contract comes together

Most contracts follow a similar path, from the client's first request to signature. On larger contracts, negotiation and internal alignment often run in parallel.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior lawyers work out the draft in detail, while senior lawyers lead the negotiation with the counterparty and set the commercial strategy.

The work is not necessarily finished after signature: the lawyer often stays involved as the contract is performed, and gets pulled back in as soon as one of the parties appears not to be honouring the agreement.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The legal profession has a fixed statutory basis for the first step: a traineeship in the Netherlands generally lasts three years and is mandatory for every starting lawyer, regardless of practice area. After that, the pace of progression depends heavily on the firm and on individual performance.

After the traineeship, the emphasis shifts from drafting contracts to independently leading matters. Making partner takes more than ten years of legal experience at most firms, and is often accompanied by a formal nomination by the existing partners.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Some lawyers move to a company after a few years, for example as in-house counsel, bringing their legal-practice experience with them.

Compensation

Compensation in the legal profession

Compensation in the legal profession usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at some firms a variable bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Commercial does not differ materially in pay structure from other practice areas at the same firm.

The ranges below are general indications for larger Dutch and international firms, and do not apply specifically to this practice area or to any of the firms named on this page. Actual pay varies by firm, region and role, and is listed where known in the individual vacancies on this site.

From partner level, the nature of compensation changes. A partner typically shares in the firm's profits rather than receiving a fixed salary, which ties pay more closely to their own client portfolio and the results of the practice group as a whole.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior lawyer

The day often starts with reviewing the counterparty's comments on a draft agreement, then working them into the document. On quieter days, much of the time goes into working out delivery, liability and termination clauses.

A commercial lawyer often works on several contracts at once, for clients across very different sectors. That requires switching quickly between matters while keeping the client's commercial interests sharply in view each time.

Just before signing, the pace shifts entirely. The lawyer closes out the last open points, aligns with the client on the final agreement, and makes sure the definitive version of the contract is ready for both parties.

Juniors

What trainee lawyers do

Junior lawyers carry much of the drafting work a contract rests on, under the supervision of a supervising partner.

  • Helping draft contracts
  • Checking terms and conditions
  • Searching contract portfolios
  • Due diligence research
  • Drafting advice memos
  • Tracking deadlines and matter status
  • Sitting in on negotiations
  • Correspondence with the counterparty

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between trainee and supervisor before a document goes to the counterparty. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior lawyer learns which clauses in a contract really matter.

Skills you will need

Legal knowledge gets the most attention in training, but the lawyers who stay longest often stand out through commercial judgement: understanding what the client is really trying to achieve with a contract, not just what it literally says.

Compare

Commercial versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in what exactly is being recorded or protected, and in which phase of the relationship the work takes place.

Commercial vs Corporate/M&A

Corporate/M&A structures the acquisition of a company itself. Commercial handles the ongoing trade contracts through which that company does business.

Commercial vs Intellectual Property

Intellectual Property protects and registers rights such as trademarks and patents. Commercial records the terms on which others may use those rights.

Commercial vs Dispute Resolution

Commercial drafts the contract. Dispute Resolution steps in once one of the parties fails to honour that contract.

Commercial vs Antitrust, Competition & Trade

Antitrust assesses whether arrangements between businesses restrict competition. Commercial separately handles the substance of the trading relationship itself.

Commercial vs Employment, Labour & Pensions

Employment governs the working relationship between an employer and employee. Commercial governs the trading relationship between two businesses.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is commercial (contract law)?

Commercial, often called contract law or trade law in the Netherlands, covers the agreements businesses use to deal with one another, such as purchase, sale, distribution and licensing. It ensures trading relationships between companies are clearly recorded in legal terms.

What is the difference between commercial and corporate/M&A?

Corporate/M&A structures the acquisition of a company itself, such as the purchase of shares or assets. Commercial handles the ongoing trade contracts a company uses day to day with suppliers, customers and partners.

What does a commercial lawyer do day to day?

Commercial lawyers draft and negotiate contracts and terms and conditions, review existing contract portfolios, flag risks before they turn into disputes, and support clients as they carry out their trading relationships.

What are terms and conditions and why do they matter?

Terms and conditions are standard provisions a business applies to all its contracts, for example on liability and payment. They matter because the law sets requirements for when they apply and when a clause can be voided.

What is a distribution or agency agreement?

A distribution agreement lets a party resell a supplier's products in its own name. An agency agreement lets a commercial agent broker sales on the supplier's behalf, with statutory protection for the agent on termination.

Which law applies to an international trade contract?

Parties can generally choose the applicable law themselves, which within the EU is governed by the Rome I Regulation. International sale contracts between businesses are also covered by the Vienna Convention, unless the parties expressly exclude it.

What skills do you need for commercial?

Precise contract drafting, commercial judgement about what the client wants to achieve, clear negotiation skills, attention to detail, and the ability to manage several matters at once under deadlines.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take electives in the law of obligations and international sale, do an internship at a firm or company with a commercial practice, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation in this field.
