Legal career path

Public Procurement

Discover what public procurement means in legal practice: how lawyers guide public authorities and companies through tender procedures, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

View vacanciesExplore legal paths

Introduction

What is public procurement?

Public procurement is the practice area setting the rules for how governments and other contracting authorities purchase works, supplies and services. Its purpose is fair, transparent and non-discriminatory access to these contracts for businesses.

A tender follows a fixed procedure: from the contract notice to the assessment of bids and the final award. Companies that disagree with an award decision can challenge it, often in summary proceedings.

Procurement lawyers advise both contracting authorities and bidding companies. The field touches administrative law because the counterparty is often the government, and competition law because fair competition is central.

What it isThe practice area covering the rules for purchasing by the government and other contracting authorities.
What it centres onSetting up, assessing and challenging tender procedures.
Why clients need itTo run a tender lawfully or to have an award decision reviewed.
Why students find it attractiveThe combination of administrative law, competition and the social relevance of public contracts.

The practice

Three settings where you practise this field

The breakdown below mainly says something about the type of client and which side of the table you sit on, not about the quality of the work.

Dutch full-service firmA procurement practice alongside the administrative law practice, often advising both authorities and bidders.
Boutique firm specialised in procurement lawFirms fully focused on procurement law and its procedures.
Legal department of a contracting authorityAn in-house role at a government organisation that runs its own tenders.

At Dutch full-service firms, the procurement practice often sits close to administrative law, with clients on both sides of the table. Boutique firms specialise fully in procurement law. At the legal department of a contracting authority, you work in-house on the tenders the organisation itself runs.

What they do

What procurement lawyers do

The work differs by matter, but the building blocks below come up in almost every tender.

Setting up tender proceduresDesigning the procedure and award criteria on behalf of a contracting authority.
Assessing bidsTesting bids against the stated requirements on behalf of the contracting authority.
Drafting tender documentsDrafting the specifications and information notes correctly under the law.
Challenging award decisionsPreparing and conducting summary proceedings against an award on behalf of a rejected bidder.
Advising on the duty to tenderAssessing whether a contract is subject to tendering and which exceptions apply.
Assessing contract amendmentsTesting whether a change during the contract term is permitted without a new tender.
Handling complaint proceduresFiling or responding to a complaint on behalf of parties before the Committee of Procurement Experts.
Coordinating with other practice groupsWorking with administrative law and competition law when a matter overlaps.

Legal framework

The key legislation

Public procurement revolves around a set of core statutes that keep recurring, even though application differs by sector and type of contract.

Dutch Public Procurement Act 2012Governs the Dutch implementation of the European procurement rules.
EU Directive 2014/24/EU (classic sectors)Governs procurement by classic public authorities within the EU.
EU Directive 2014/25/EU (utilities sectors)Governs procurement in the water, energy, transport and postal sectors.
EU Remedies DirectivesGovern the legal protection of bidders against unlawful award decisions.
Uniform Administrative Conditions for Works (ARW)Contains additional procedural rules for tenders for works.
Guide to ProportionalityContains guidelines on proportionate requirements and criteria in tenders.

Step by step

How a tender procedure unfolds

Most public tenders follow a similar path, from the contract notice to the final award. For more complex contracts, assessing bids can take considerable time.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior lawyers work out the tender documents and assessments in detail, while senior lawyers set strategy and conduct summary proceedings.

Not every tender proceeds unopposed: a rejected bidder can start summary proceedings during the standstill period, which can significantly delay the final award.

Roles

Roles in the procurement practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from drafting documents toward setting procurement strategy and conducting summary proceedings. Most lawyers climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving between firms at any level is common.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The legal profession has a fixed statutory basis for the first step: a traineeship in the Netherlands generally lasts three years and is mandatory for every starting lawyer, regardless of practice area. After that, the pace of progression depends heavily on the firm and on individual performance.

After the traineeship, the emphasis shifts from drafting documents to independently leading tender matters and conducting summary proceedings. Making partner takes more than ten years of legal experience at most firms.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Some lawyers move into a legal role at a contracting authority, bringing their experience with them.

LevelTypical time in role

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior lawyer

The day often starts with reviewing tender specifications or preparing advice on whether a contract is subject to tendering. On days without a hearing, much of the time goes into drafting tender documents and assessing bids.

A procurement lawyer often works on several procedures at once, each with its own deadlines fixed tightly in the procurement rules. That requires strict planning, since a missed deadline can invalidate a bid.

On a day with summary proceedings, the pace shifts entirely. The lawyer prepares the pleading, anticipates the other side, and supports the client during the hearing.

Juniors

What trainee lawyers do

Junior lawyers carry much of the research and drafting work a procurement opinion or proceeding rests on, under the supervision of a supervising partner.

  • Helping draft tender documents
  • Drafting draft advice
  • Case law and literature research
  • Checking bids against stated requirements
  • Preparing information notes
  • Tracking procedural deadlines
  • Sitting in on hearings
  • Correspondence with the counterparty

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between trainee and supervisor before a document is filed. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior lawyer learns which requirements in a tender really matter.

Compare

Public Procurement versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in who the client is and which part of the process is at the centre.

Public Procurement vs Administrative Law

Administrative Law covers the broader legal relationship between a citizen or business and the government. Public Procurement focuses specifically on a contracting authority's purchasing procedure.

Public Procurement vs Commercial

Commercial drafts the contract after award. Public Procurement oversees the process that precedes that contract.

Public Procurement vs Dispute Resolution

Dispute Resolution conducts the broader procedural law. Public Procurement applies that procedural law specifically to disputes over an award decision.

Public Procurement vs Antitrust, Competition & Trade

Antitrust protects fair competition between businesses generally. Public Procurement protects fair competition specifically in public contracts.

Public Procurement vs ESG

ESG covers sustainability regulation broadly. Public Procurement touches this specifically through sustainable public purchasing by contracting authorities.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is public procurement?

Public procurement is the practice area setting the rules for how governments and other contracting authorities purchase works, supplies and services.

Why does procurement law exist?

Procurement law ensures fair, transparent and non-discriminatory access to public contracts, so that businesses from across the EU can compete on equal terms.

What does a procurement lawyer do day to day?

Procurement lawyers set up tender procedures, draft tender documents, assess bids, conduct summary proceedings against award decisions, and advise on the duty to tender.

What is a standstill period?

The standstill period is the mandatory waiting time between the provisional and final award, during which a rejected bidder can challenge the award decision, for example in summary proceedings.

What is the difference between classic sectors and utilities sectors?

Classic sectors concern regular public contracts for works, supplies and services. Utilities sectors concern tenders in water, energy, transport and postal services, with their own EU directive and partly different rules.

What is the Committee of Procurement Experts?

The Committee of Procurement Experts handles complaints about tender procedures as a low-threshold alternative to going to court.

What skills do you need for public procurement?

Precise writing, analytical thinking, procedural instinct, attention to detail, and the ability to work under the pressure of tight tender deadlines.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take electives in administrative law or European law, do an internship at a firm or contracting authority, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation in this field.
