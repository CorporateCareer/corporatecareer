Legal career path

Dispute Resolution

Discover what dispute resolution means in legal practice: how lawyers conduct proceedings before courts and arbitrators, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

View vacanciesExplore legal paths

Introduction

What is dispute resolution?

Dispute resolution, often called litigation or procedural law in the Netherlands, is the practice area centred on resolving legal conflicts: through the courts, through arbitration, or through mediation. Where most other practice areas aim to prevent disputes, dispute resolution steps in precisely when parties can no longer agree.

A proceeding starts with a writ of summons or a petition and then follows a fixed procedural path: exchanging submissions, presenting evidence, an oral hearing, and eventually a judgment. In arbitration and mediation, the process is less fixed and parties have more influence over its form.

Dispute resolution lawyers often work with the practice group the underlying dispute comes from: a contract dispute touches commercial, a shareholder dispute touches corporate/M&A, and a dispute with the government touches administrative law.

What it isThe practice area covering the resolution of legal conflicts, through the courts, arbitration or mediation.
What it centres onBuilding a case file, conducting proceedings, and tracking procedural deadlines.
Why clients need itTo resolve a dispute with a counterparty effectively and with a good outcome.
Why students find it attractiveVisible hearing moments, sharp legal argumentation, and direct contact with the court.

What they do

What dispute resolution lawyers do

The work differs by proceeding, but the building blocks below come up in almost every dispute. A lawyer often works on several proceedings at once, each with its own deadlines.

Building the case fileStructuring facts, evidence and legal arguments for a proceeding.
Drafting the writ of summons or petitionDrafting the submission that formally starts a proceeding.
Pleading before the courtPresenting the client's position orally during a hearing.
Coordinating evidenceUsing witnesses, experts and documents to substantiate a claim.
Conducting settlement negotiationsAssessing whether an amicable solution is preferable to continued litigation.
Guiding arbitration proceedingsHaving disputes resolved by arbitrators instead of the state court.
Preparing summary proceedingsSetting up an urgent proceeding when a ruling cannot wait.
Coordinating with other practice groupsWorking with the practice group the underlying dispute stems from.

Legal framework

The key legislation

Dispute resolution revolves around a limited set of core rules from procedural law that keep recurring, even though the substance differs greatly from case to case.

Dutch Code of Civil ProcedureGoverns civil procedural law: how a proceeding is started and conducted.
Books 3 and 6 of the Dutch Civil CodeThe substantive rules on obligations that a claim is often based on.
Arbitration Act (Book 4 of the Code of Civil Procedure)Governs arbitration proceedings as an alternative to the state court.
Mediation ActGoverns the quality safeguards around mediation as a form of dispute resolution.
Brussels I bis RegulationEU rules on jurisdiction and recognition of judgments in cross-border disputes.
Act on the Settlement of Mass Damages in Collective Actions (WAMCA)Governs collective damages claims on behalf of large groups of affected parties.

Practice areas

The main sub-areas

Dispute resolution is broad, and most lawyers specialise within one of the sub-areas below.

Civil proceedingsDisputes between businesses or individuals before the civil court.
ArbitrationDispute resolution by an arbitrator instead of the state court, often in international contracts.
MediationGuided negotiation aimed at a solution without a court proceeding.
Summary proceedingsUrgent proceedings where a provisional ruling is needed quickly.
Collective actionsMass claims on behalf of large groups of affected parties, often against large companies.

Step by step

How a civil proceeding unfolds

Most civil proceedings follow a similar path, from the writ of summons to the judgment. In more complex cases, presenting evidence can take considerable time.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior lawyers build the case file and work out submissions in detail, while senior lawyers plead and discuss procedural risks with the client.

Not every proceeding ends in a judgment: parties can still settle at any point, which is often faster and cheaper than litigating all the way through a possible appeal.

Roles

Roles in the dispute resolution practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from building the case file toward setting litigation strategy and pleading before the court. Most lawyers climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving between firms at any level is common.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The legal profession has a fixed statutory basis for the first step: a traineeship in the Netherlands generally lasts three years and is mandatory for every starting lawyer, regardless of practice area. After that, the pace of progression depends heavily on the firm and on individual performance.

After the traineeship, the emphasis shifts from building case files to independently leading proceedings and pleading before the court. Making partner takes more than ten years of legal experience at most firms.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Some lawyers move into the judiciary, for example as a deputy judge, bringing their litigation experience with them.

Advocaat / Associate3 to 5 years

Compensation

Compensation in the legal profession

Compensation in the legal profession usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at some firms a variable bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Dispute resolution does not differ materially in pay structure from other practice areas at the same firm.

The ranges below are general indications for larger Dutch and international firms, and do not apply specifically to this practice area or to any of the firms named on this page. Actual pay varies by firm, region and role, and is listed where known in the individual vacancies on this site.

From partner level, the nature of compensation changes. A partner typically shares in the firm's profits rather than receiving a fixed salary, which ties pay more closely to their own client portfolio and the results of the practice group as a whole.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior lawyer

The day often starts with reviewing the counterparty's submission or preparing a defence. On days without a hearing, much of the time goes into drafting submissions and searching through evidence.

A dispute resolution lawyer often works on several proceedings at once, each with its own deadlines set by the court. That requires strict planning, since missing a procedural deadline can have major consequences for the client.

On a hearing day, the pace shifts entirely. The lawyer prepares the pleading, anticipates questions from the judge, and supports the client during the oral hearing.

Juniors

What trainee lawyers do

Junior lawyers carry much of the research and drafting work a proceeding rests on, under the supervision of a supervising partner.

  • Building the case file
  • Drafting draft submissions
  • Case law and literature research
  • Searching through evidence
  • Drafting advice memos
  • Tracking procedural deadlines
  • Sitting in on hearings
  • Correspondence with the counterparty

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between trainee and supervisor before a submission is filed. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior lawyer learns which arguments in a proceeding really matter.

Skills

Skills you will need

Legal knowledge gets the most attention in training, but the lawyers who stay longest often stand out through persuasiveness: being able to present a complex story clearly and convincingly, both on paper and orally before the court.

Compare

Dispute Resolution versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in who the counterparty is, and whether the work is about preventing or resolving a dispute.

Dispute Resolution vs Corporate/M&A

Corporate/M&A guides the healthy formation of an acquisition. Dispute Resolution steps in once parties disagree after the deal.

Dispute Resolution vs Commercial

Commercial drafts the contract. Dispute Resolution steps in once one of the parties fails to honour that contract.

Dispute Resolution vs Restructuring & Insolvency

Restructuring & Insolvency deals with a company's own financial difficulties. Dispute Resolution deals with a specific legal disagreement between parties.

Dispute Resolution vs Administrative Law

Administrative Law covers disputes between a citizen or business and the government. Dispute Resolution handles disputes between private parties.

Dispute Resolution vs Employment, Labour & Pensions

Employment covers disputes within an employment relationship. Dispute Resolution is the broader procedural law also applied to other types of disputes.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is dispute resolution?

Dispute resolution, often called litigation or procedural law in the Netherlands, is the practice area centred on resolving legal conflicts through the courts, arbitration or mediation.

What is the difference between arbitration and court proceedings?

In court proceedings, the state court resolves the dispute under the Code of Civil Procedure. In arbitration, parties choose one or more arbitrators to resolve the dispute themselves, often faster and more confidentially, based on an arbitration clause in their contract.

What does a dispute resolution lawyer do day to day?

Dispute resolution lawyers build case files, draft submissions, plead before the court, conduct settlement negotiations, and guide clients through arbitration or mediation proceedings.

What are summary proceedings?

Summary proceedings are an urgent procedure in which the court quickly issues a provisional ruling, for example to prevent an imminent violation of rights. A definitive ruling may follow later in a proceedings on the merits.

What is mediation and when is it used?

Mediation is a guided negotiation between parties led by an independent mediator, aimed at a solution without a court proceeding. It is often used when parties want to preserve an ongoing relationship.

What is a collective action (WAMCA)?

A collective action is a proceeding in which an interest organisation files a claim on behalf of a large group of affected parties, for example against a company. The WAMCA sets out the conditions under which such a collective damages claim is possible.

What skills do you need for dispute resolution?

Precise writing, persuasiveness, strong oral presentation, attention to detail, and the ability to work under the pressure of procedural deadlines.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take electives in civil procedural law, take part in a pleading competition, do an internship at a firm with a dispute resolution practice, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation in this field.
