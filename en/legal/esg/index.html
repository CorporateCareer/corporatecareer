ESG

Discover what ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) means in legal practice: how lawyers guide companies through sustainability regulation, human rights and corporate governance, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

Introduction

What is ESG in legal practice?

ESG stands for Environmental, Social and Governance and, in legal practice, is the field centred on regulation around sustainability, human rights in the supply chain, and good corporate governance. It is not a single code of law, but a fast-growing body of regulation that increasingly affects companies directly.

The E stands for environment and climate, the S for human rights and working conditions in the supply chain, and the G for how a company is governed and overseen. Lawyers in this field help companies meet the reporting and due diligence obligations that flow from these three themes.

ESG lawyers often work with other practice groups: an ESG issue in an acquisition touches corporate/M&A, a climate dispute touches dispute resolution, and the social side of ESG touches employment, labour & pensions.

What it isThe practice area covering sustainability regulation, supply chain human rights and corporate governance.
What it centres onAdvising on reporting obligations, supply chain responsibility and governance structures.
Why clients need itTo comply with fast-growing European and Dutch sustainability legislation.
Why students find it attractiveA new and fast-changing field with direct social relevance.

Legal framework

The key legislation

ESG revolves around a fast-growing set of European and Dutch rules that keep recurring, even though application differs greatly from company to company.

Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)EU directive requiring large companies to produce extensive sustainability reporting.
Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)EU directive requiring companies to carry out human rights and environmental due diligence in the supply chain.
EU Taxonomy RegulationClassifies which economic activities count as environmentally sustainable.
Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR)Requires financial market participants to disclose sustainability risks.
Book 2 of the Dutch Civil CodeContains the provisions on management, supervision and accountability within legal entities.
Dutch Corporate Governance CodeContains principles and best practices for the good governance of listed companies.

Step by step

How a CSRD reporting cycle unfolds

Most sustainability reporting cycles follow a similar path, from the materiality assessment to publication. At more complex companies, gathering data can take considerable time.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior lawyers map the underlying regulation and data, while senior lawyers set the reporting strategy and advise the board.

Not every cycle runs smoothly: an incomplete materiality assessment or missing data can cause delays further down the process, which often costs more time than careful preparation upfront.

Roles

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from mapping regulation toward setting ESG strategy and advising the board. Most lawyers climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving between firms at any level is common.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The legal profession has a fixed statutory basis for the first step: a traineeship in the Netherlands generally lasts three years and is mandatory for every starting lawyer, regardless of practice area. After that, the pace of progression depends heavily on the firm and on individual performance.

After the traineeship, the emphasis shifts from mapping regulation to independently advising on ESG strategy. Making partner takes more than ten years of legal experience at most firms.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Some lawyers move into an in-house sustainability role at a company, bringing their ESG experience with them.

LevelTypical time in role

Advocaat / Associate3 to 5 years
Senior Associate2 to 4 years

Compensation

Compensation in the legal profession

Compensation in the legal profession usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at some firms a variable bonus tied to individual and firm performance. ESG does not differ materially in pay structure from other practice areas at the same firm.

The ranges below are general indications for larger Dutch and international firms, and do not apply specifically to this practice area or to any particular firm. Actual pay varies by firm, region and role, and is listed where known in the individual vacancies on this site.

From partner level, the nature of compensation changes. A partner typically shares in the firm's profits rather than receiving a fixed salary, which ties pay more closely to their own client portfolio and the results of the practice group as a whole.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior lawyer

The day often starts with reviewing new or amended regulation or preparing advice on a reporting obligation. On days without a client meeting, much of the time goes into drafting compliance documents and searching through source data.

An ESG lawyer often works on several matters at once, from a reporting cycle to a supply chain due diligence. That requires strict planning, since ESG legislation changes fast and deadlines are often set at EU level.

On a day with a client meeting, the pace shifts entirely. The lawyer prepares the advice, anticipates questions from the board, and explains complex regulation in plain terms.

Juniors

What trainee lawyers do

Junior lawyers carry much of the research and drafting work an ESG opinion rests on, under the supervision of a supervising partner.

  • Mapping regulation
  • Drafting draft advice
  • Searching through data and source documents
  • Drafting compliance checklists
  • Preparing due diligence questionnaires
  • Tracking reporting deadlines
  • Sitting in on client meetings
  • Correspondence with regulators

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between trainee and supervisor before advice is sent. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior lawyer learns which regulation in an ESG matter really matters.

Compare

ESG versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in which part of the company or dispute is at the centre.

ESG vs Corporate/M&A

Corporate/M&A guides the transaction itself. ESG assesses the sustainability and governance risks that come with that transaction.

ESG vs Commercial

Commercial drafts the contract. ESG tests whether the sustainability claims and clauses within it hold up.

ESG vs Administrative Law

Administrative Law handles disputes with the government, for example over environmental permits. ESG advises more broadly on sustainability regulation towards the company itself.

ESG vs Employment, Labour & Pensions

Employment covers the employment relationship itself. ESG covers a company's broader social responsibility across its supply chain.

ESG vs Dispute Resolution

Dispute Resolution conducts the proceeding once a climate or sustainability dispute escalates. ESG focuses on preventing that through compliance.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is ESG?

ESG stands for Environmental, Social and Governance and is the practice area centred on sustainability regulation, human rights in the supply chain, and corporate governance.

What is the difference between the CSRD and the CSDDD?

The CSRD requires large companies to produce extensive sustainability reporting. The CSDDD requires companies to carry out due diligence on human rights and the environment in their own supply chain. Both are EU directives that are often handled together.

What does an ESG lawyer do day to day?

ESG lawyers advise on sustainability reporting, carry out human rights and environmental due diligence, advise on corporate governance, and set up compliance programmes.

What is greenwashing and why does it matter to ESG lawyers?

Greenwashing is making misleading sustainability claims. ESG lawyers review marketing statements to help prevent a company from doing this, which can otherwise lead to reputational damage and legal liability.

What does corporate governance mean within ESG?

Corporate governance concerns how a company is managed and overseen, including the responsibilities of management and supervisory bodies and the interests of different stakeholders.

What skills do you need for ESG?

Analytical thinking, precise writing, adaptability, attention to detail, and the ability to translate fast-changing regulation into concrete advice.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take electives in corporate law or environmental law with a focus on sustainability, do an internship at a firm or sustainability department, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation in this field.
