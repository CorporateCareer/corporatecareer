Employment, Labour & Pensions

Discover what employment, labour & pensions means in legal practice: how lawyers guide employment relationships, works councils and pension schemes, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

View vacanciesExplore legal paths

Introduction

What is employment, labour & pensions?

Employment, labour & pensions is the practice area centred on the relationship between employer and employee: from drafting an employment contract to guiding a dismissal, and from employee representation within a company to the pension scheme that goes with it.

The field combines individual employment law, such as contracts and dismissal proceedings, with collective labour law, such as collective bargaining and the role of the works council. Pensions law is an additional specialism often housed within the same practice group.

Employer-side lawyers advise companies on their workforce policy, while in reorganisations and acquisitions this practice area works closely with corporate/M&A and restructuring & insolvency.

What it isThe practice area covering the relationship between employer and employee, from contract to pension.
What it centres onAdvising on employment terms, dismissal, employee representation and pension schemes.
Why clients need itTo ground workforce policy legally and to prevent or resolve employment disputes.
Why students find it attractiveDirect human contact, broad social relevance, and a mix of advisory work and litigation.

The practice

Three settings where you practise this field

The breakdown below mainly says something about the type of client and the scale of matters, not about the quality of the work.

Dutch full-service firmAn employment law practice alongside other practice groups, often brought in during reorganisations and acquisitions.
Boutique firm specialised in employment lawFirms fully focused on employment law, often with deep specialisation in complex dismissal matters.
Regional firm or legal expense insurerHandles a high volume of individual employment matters, often on behalf of employees or SMEs.

At Dutch full-service firms, the employment practice often sits close to corporate/M&A, since acquisitions and reorganisations affect staff. Boutique firms specialise fully in employment law and build deep expertise as a result. At a regional firm or legal expense insurer, you handle a larger volume of individual matters, often on behalf of employees.

What they do

What employment lawyers do

The work differs by matter, but the building blocks below come up in almost every employment law question.

Drafting and reviewing employment contractsStructuring individual employment agreements and terms of employment.
Guiding dismissal proceedingsPreparing and conducting a termination of employment via the UWV or the sub-district court.
Negotiating reorganisations and social plansAligning collective redundancy and its conditions with trade unions and the works council.
Advising on employee representationAdvising companies on the role and rights of the works council.
Advising on pension schemesAdvising employers and pension funds on the design and amendment of pension schemes.
Supporting collective bargainingGuiding employers through the negotiation of collective labour agreements.
Handling employment disputesAssessing disputes over performance, integrity or terms of employment.
Guiding labour migrationAdvising employers on employing staff from abroad.

Legal framework

The key legislation

Employment, labour & pensions revolves around a limited set of core rules that keep recurring, even though the substance differs greatly from matter to matter.

Book 7, Title 10 of the Dutch Civil CodeGoverns the individual employment contract: rights, obligations and dismissal protection.
Balanced Labour Market Act (WAB)Governs the balance between permanent and flexible employment contracts and dismissal routes.
Works Councils Act (WOR)Governs employee representation through the works council.
Collective Labour Agreement ActGoverns the status and operation of collective labour agreements.
Pensions ActGoverns the design, administration and amendment of pension schemes.
General Equal Treatment Act (AWGB)Prohibits discrimination in the labour market and the employment relationship.

Step by step

How a dismissal proceeding unfolds

Most dismissal proceedings follow a similar path, from the application to the ruling. In more complex cases, the hearing and the negotiation that follows can take considerable time.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior lawyers build the file and work out submissions in detail, while senior lawyers negotiate and discuss procedural risks with the client.

Not every proceeding ends in a ruling: parties can still reach a settlement agreement at any point, which is often faster and more certain than litigating all the way through a possible appeal.

Roles

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The legal profession has a fixed statutory basis for the first step: a traineeship in the Netherlands generally lasts three years and is mandatory for every starting lawyer, regardless of practice area. After that, the pace of progression depends heavily on the firm and on individual performance.

After the traineeship, the emphasis shifts from building files to independently leading negotiations and proceedings. Making partner takes more than ten years of legal experience at most firms.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Some lawyers move into an HR role or an in-house counsel position, bringing their employment law experience with them.

Compensation

Compensation in the legal profession

Compensation in the legal profession usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at some firms a variable bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Employment, labour & pensions does not differ materially in pay structure from other practice areas at the same firm.

The ranges below are general indications for larger Dutch and international firms, and do not apply specifically to this practice area or to any of the firms named on this page. Actual pay varies by firm, region and role, and is listed where known in the individual vacancies on this site.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior lawyer

The day often starts with reviewing an employment contract or preparing advice on a difficult personnel matter. On days without a meeting, much of the time goes into drafting contracts and answering client questions.

An employment lawyer often works on several matters at once, from an individual dismissal to an ongoing reorganisation. That requires strict planning, since missing a deadline at the UWV or the sub-district court can have major consequences for the client.

On a day with a hearing or negotiation, the pace shifts entirely. The lawyer prepares the position, anticipates the other side, and supports the client during the meeting or hearing.

Juniors

What trainee lawyers do

Junior lawyers carry much of the research and drafting work advice or a proceeding rests on, under the supervision of a supervising partner.

  • Helping draft employment contracts
  • Drafting draft advice
  • Case law and literature research
  • Searching through personnel files
  • Drafting draft submissions
  • Tracking procedural deadlines
  • Sitting in on meetings and hearings
  • Correspondence with the counterparty

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between trainee and supervisor before advice or a submission is sent. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior lawyer learns which arguments in an employment law matter really matter.

Compare

Employment, Labour & Pensions versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in who the counterparty is, and whether the focus is the company itself or its workforce.

Employment vs Corporate/M&A

Corporate/M&A guides the acquisition itself. Employment assesses what happens to staff when the company transfers to a new owner.

Employment vs Restructuring & Insolvency

Restructuring & Insolvency deals with a company's financial difficulties. Employment handles what specifically happens to employment contracts in a collective redundancy.

Employment vs Dispute Resolution

Dispute Resolution is the broader procedural law for all kinds of disputes. Employment applies that procedural law specifically to disputes within the employment relationship.

Employment vs Administrative Law

Administrative Law covers the legal position of civil servants vis-a-vis the government as employer. Employment covers the employment relationship in the private sector.

Employment vs Commercial

Commercial covers contracts between companies. Employment covers the contractual relationship between a company and its own staff.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is employment, labour & pensions?

Employment, labour & pensions is the practice area centred on the relationship between employer and employee, from employment contracts and dismissal to employee representation and pension schemes.

What is the difference between individual and collective labour law?

Individual labour law concerns the relationship between one employer and one employee, such as an employment contract or dismissal. Collective labour law concerns agreements that apply to larger groups of employees, such as a collective labour agreement or the role of the works council.

What does an employment lawyer do day to day?

Employment lawyers draft employment contracts, guide dismissal proceedings, advise on employee representation and pension schemes, support collective bargaining, and handle employment disputes.

What is the Balanced Labour Market Act (WAB)?

The WAB is a law that governs the balance between permanent and flexible employment contracts and the associated dismissal routes. It has been amended regularly and remains a key point of attention in employment law practice.

What is the role of the works council?

The works council represents staff and has advisory and consent rights over major employer decisions, such as reorganisations, set out in the Works Councils Act.

What does pensions law involve within this field?

Pensions law concerns the legal design, administration and amendment of pension schemes, and is often handled within the same practice group as employment law.

What skills do you need for this field?

Precise writing, negotiation skills, people skills, attention to detail, and the ability to work under the pressure of procedural deadlines.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take electives in employment law, do an internship at a firm or HR department, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation in this field.
