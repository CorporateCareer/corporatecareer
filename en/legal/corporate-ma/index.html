Corporate/M&A

Discover what corporate/M&A means in legal practice: how lawyers guide mergers and acquisitions, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

Introduction

What is corporate/M&A?

Corporate/M&A, often called corporate law or mergers and acquisitions in the Netherlands, is the practice area that guides the purchase and sale of companies: from the first negotiation to the final transfer of shares or assets. It is one of the most visible and sought-after specialisations in the legal profession.

In an acquisition, the lawyer works out the transaction structure, coordinates the due diligence investigation into the target company, and negotiates the purchase agreement, the SPA (Share Purchase Agreement) or APA (Asset Purchase Agreement). The broader field of corporate law also falls under this practice area: incorporating companies, corporate governance, and the legal structure of a business.

Corporate/M&A lawyers work closely with other practice groups: banking and finance for the acquisition financing, competition law for the merger filing, and tax law for structuring the deal.

What it isThe practice area covering the purchase, sale and legal structure of companies.
What it centres onThe transaction structure, the due diligence investigation and the negotiation of the purchase agreement.
Why clients need itTo complete an acquisition, merger or restructuring in a legally sound, risk-aware way.
Why students find it attractiveVisible deal moments, international transactions, and a direct view of the entire acquisition process.

The practice

Three settings where you practise this field

The breakdown below mainly says something about the scale and type of transaction you work on, not about the quality of the work.

International firmFirms that guide large, often cross-border acquisitions and IPOs.
Dutch full-service firmA corporate/M&A practice that often forms the core of the firm, alongside banking & finance and dispute resolution.
Boutique or regional firmSmaller, often specialised teams that serve the SME segment and mid-sized transactions.

At international firms you work on large, often cross-border deals with dozens of advisors at once. At Dutch full-service firms, corporate/M&A often forms the core of the practice, with a strong link to banking and finance. At boutique and regional firms you work on smaller-scale, often domestic transactions, taking on more responsibility earlier in your career.

What they do

What corporate/M&A lawyers do

The work differs by deal, but the building blocks below come up in almost every transaction. A lawyer often works on several acquisitions at once, each at a different stage.

Working out the transaction structureDetermining whether an acquisition is best structured as a share deal or an asset deal.
Coordinating due diligenceMapping the legal, financial and operational risks of the target company.
Drafting the purchase agreementDrafting and negotiating the SPA or APA, including warranties and indemnities.
Setting up the corporate structureShaping the legal structure of the company before and after the transaction.
Negotiating with the counterpartyNegotiating price, warranties and other contract terms on the client's behalf.
Coordinating closingBringing together all parties, documents and signatures on completion day.
Corporate governance adviceAdvising boards and shareholders on powers and responsibilities.
Coordinating with other practice groupsWorking with financing, competition and tax specialists on the same deal.

Legal framework

The key legislation

Corporate/M&A revolves around a limited set of core rules from corporate law that keep recurring, even though the substance differs greatly from deal to deal.

Book 2 of the Dutch Civil CodeGoverns the legal form, management and governance of legal entities such as the bv and nv.
Book 7 of the Dutch Civil Code (sale)The general rules on purchase agreements, also applicable to share transactions.
SER Merger CodeRules on information and consultation obligations towards employees and unions in a merger.
Collective Redundancy Notification ActApplies when an acquisition is accompanied by a reorganisation.
Competition Act (merger control)Larger acquisitions must be notified in advance to the ACM or the European Commission.
Vifo ActScreens acquisitions in vital sectors for risks to national security.

Corporate/M&A is broad, and most lawyers specialise within one of the sub-areas below.

Step by step

How an acquisition comes together

Most acquisitions follow a similar path, from the first term sheet to closing. On larger deals, due diligence and negotiation often run in parallel.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior lawyers carry out the due diligence investigation and work out the documentation in detail, while senior lawyers lead the negotiation and set the deal structure.

Closing is often the most tense moment: every condition precedent must be checked off, every signature must be in, and only then is the transfer actually completed.

Roles

Roles in the corporate/M&A practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from carrying out due diligence toward setting deal strategy and managing the client relationship. Most lawyers climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving between firms at any level is common.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The legal profession has a fixed statutory basis for the first step: a traineeship in the Netherlands generally lasts three years and is mandatory for every starting lawyer, regardless of practice area. After that, the pace of progression depends heavily on the firm and on individual performance.

After the traineeship, the emphasis shifts from carrying out due diligence to independently leading transactions. Making partner takes more than ten years of legal experience at most firms, and is often accompanied by a formal nomination by the existing partners.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Some lawyers move into business after a few years, for example as in-house counsel in a company's corporate department.

LevelTypical time in role

Advocaat / Associate3 to 5 years
Senior Associate2 to 4 years
Partnertop level, 10+ years in total

Compensation

Compensation in the legal profession

Compensation in the legal profession usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at some firms a variable bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Corporate/M&A does not differ materially in pay structure from other practice areas at the same firm.

The ranges below are general indications for larger Dutch and international firms, and do not apply specifically to this practice area or to any of the firms named on this page. Actual pay varies by firm, region and role, and is listed where known in the individual vacancies on this site.

From partner level, the nature of compensation changes. A partner typically shares in the firm's profits rather than receiving a fixed salary, which ties pay more closely to their own client portfolio and the results of the practice group as a whole.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior lawyer

The day often starts with reviewing due diligence findings or the counterparty's comments on a draft purchase agreement. On quieter days, much of the time goes into working out warranties, indemnities and the other SPA provisions.

A corporate/M&A lawyer often works on several transactions at once, each at a different stage. That requires switching quickly between matters and keeping track of dozens of open action points per deal.

On completion day, the pace shifts entirely. The lawyer closes out the last conditions precedent, chases missing signatures, and coordinates with all parties on the exact moment of transfer.

Juniors

What trainee lawyers do

Junior lawyers carry much of the research and drafting work a transaction rests on, under the supervision of a supervising partner.

  • Carrying out due diligence research
  • Drafting draft purchase agreements
  • Preparing corporate documentation
  • Managing the data room
  • Drafting advice memos
  • Tracking deadlines and action points
  • Sitting in on negotiations
  • Correspondence with the counterparty

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between trainee and supervisor before a document goes to the counterparty. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior lawyer learns which clauses in a purchase agreement really matter.

Skills

Skills you will need

Legal knowledge gets the most attention in training, but the lawyers who stay longest often stand out through commercial judgement: understanding what the client is really trying to achieve with a transaction, not just what the contract literally says.

Analytical thinking Precise writing Commercial judgement Attention to detail Negotiation Project management Client focus Working under deadlines Legal English Persuasiveness

Getting in

How you get into corporate/M&A

Most routes run through a law master's with a specialisation in corporate law, followed by an internship or traineeship at a firm. An affinity with deal work and numbers is a plus, but not a requirement.

Master's in corporate lawThe most direct route, with courses that tie directly into the practice.
Internship during the master'sGaining practical experience at a firm before graduating.
Traineeship after graduatingStructured programmes in which you rotate through several practice groups.
Moot courts on corporate lawPractising negotiation and deal documentation outside the regular curriculum.

Publishing in legal journalsA case note or article on corporate law stands out in applications.

Compare

Corporate/M&A versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in what exactly is being arranged, and in which phase of the transaction the work takes place.

Corporate/M&A vs Banking & Finance

Corporate/M&A structures the acquisition itself. Banking & finance arranges the loan the buyer uses to pay for that acquisition.

Corporate/M&A vs Commercial

Corporate/M&A handles the one-off acquisition of a company. Commercial handles the ongoing trade contracts through which that company does business.

Corporate/M&A vs Antitrust, Competition & Trade

Antitrust assesses whether an acquisition harms competition. Corporate/M&A separately structures the transaction itself.

Corporate/M&A vs Tax

Tax law assesses the tax consequences of a transaction structure. Corporate/M&A handles the underlying purchase agreement itself.

Corporate/M&A vs Restructuring & Insolvency

Corporate/M&A guides the healthy sale of a company. Restructuring & Insolvency steps in once a company is in financial difficulty.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is corporate/M&A?

Corporate/M&A, often called corporate law or mergers and acquisitions in the Netherlands, is the practice area that guides the purchase and sale of companies, from the first negotiation to the transfer of shares or assets.

What is the difference between a share deal and an asset deal?

In a share deal, the buyer purchases the shares of the company, including all its assets and liabilities. In an asset deal, the buyer purchases specific assets and possibly liabilities, without taking over the company itself.

What does a corporate/M&A lawyer do day to day?

Corporate/M&A lawyers coordinate due diligence, draft and negotiate purchase agreements, structure the transaction, and support clients through closing.

What is due diligence and why does it matter?

Due diligence is the investigation into the legal, financial and operational risks of the target company before an acquisition is completed. It helps the buyer identify unexpected risks and address them in the purchase agreement.

What is an SPA?

An SPA (Share Purchase Agreement) or APA (Asset Purchase Agreement) is the purchase agreement that sets out the terms of an acquisition, including price, warranties, indemnities and conditions precedent.

When must an acquisition be notified to the ACM?

Larger acquisitions must be notified in advance to the Authority for Consumers and Markets or the European Commission if the companies involved exceed certain turnover thresholds, before the transaction may be completed.

What skills do you need for corporate/M&A?

Precise contract drafting, commercial judgement, clear negotiation skills, attention to detail, and the ability to manage several transactions at once under deadlines.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take electives in corporate law, do an internship at a firm with a corporate/M&A practice, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation in this field.
