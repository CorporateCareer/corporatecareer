Legal career path

Real Estate

Discover what real estate means in legal practice: how lawyers guide property transactions, leasing relationships and area development, what they do day to day, the career ladder, and how students prepare for an internship or traineeship.

View vacanciesExplore legal paths

Introduction

What is real estate law?

Real estate law is the practice area centred on the purchase, sale, lease, financing and development of property. It combines property law, tenancy law and administrative law into a practice serving both individual and large-scale commercial property transactions.

A property transaction usually follows a fixed path: from the letter of intent and due diligence to the purchase agreement and the notarial transfer. Leasing follows different rules, with varying protection for tenants of residential and commercial space.

Real estate lawyers often work with other practice groups: a property transaction within an acquisition touches corporate/M&A, financing touches banking & finance, and a dispute over a lease touches dispute resolution.

What it isThe practice area covering the purchase, sale, lease, financing and development of property.
What it centres onStructuring, drafting and guiding property transactions and leasing relationships.
Why clients need itTo structure a property transaction or leasing relationship correctly and with an eye on risk.
Why students find it attractiveTangible transactions with a physical, visible outcome and plenty of contact with different parties.

The work differs by matter, but the building blocks below come up in almost every property transaction.

Legal framework

The key legislation

Real estate revolves around a set of core statutes that keep recurring, even though application differs greatly between transactions, leasing and area development.

Book 5 of the Dutch Civil CodeGoverns ownership and other property rights, such as ground lease and building rights.
Book 7, Title 4 of the Dutch Civil CodeGoverns leasing, with separate protection regimes for residential and commercial space.
Environment and Planning ActGoverns permits and rules for the physical environment, including construction and zoning plans.
Land Registry ActGoverns the registration of ownership and property rights in the public registers.
Municipal Pre-emption Rights ActGives municipalities a right of first refusal on the sale of certain designated land under certain conditions.
Legal Transactions Tax ActGoverns the transfer tax due on the acquisition of real property.

Real estate is broad, and most lawyers specialise within one of the sub-areas below.

Step by step

How a property transaction unfolds

Most commercial property transactions follow a similar path, from the letter of intent to registration with the Land Registry. For more complex portfolios, due diligence can take considerable time.

Who does which step depends on seniority. Junior lawyers carry out due diligence and work out the purchase agreement in detail, while senior lawyers lead negotiations and set the transaction structure.

Not every transaction runs smoothly: findings from due diligence can lead to renegotiating the price or terms before the purchase agreement is signed.

Roles

Roles in the real estate practice

Each step up the ladder shifts the emphasis further from carrying out due diligence toward setting transaction strategy and leading negotiations. Most lawyers climb this ladder at the same firm, though moving between firms at any level is common.

Career timeline

How long each step takes

The legal profession has a fixed statutory basis for the first step: a traineeship in the Netherlands generally lasts three years and is mandatory for every starting lawyer, regardless of practice area. After that, the pace of progression depends heavily on the firm and on individual performance.

After the traineeship, the emphasis shifts from carrying out due diligence to independently leading transactions and negotiations. Making partner takes more than ten years of legal experience at most firms.

Not everyone climbs the whole ladder at the same firm. Some lawyers move into a role at a property investor or developer, bringing their experience with them.

Advocaat / Associate3 to 5 years
Senior Associate2 to 4 years

Compensation

Compensation in the legal profession

Compensation in the legal profession usually consists of a fixed salary that rises with seniority, and at some firms a variable bonus tied to individual and firm performance. Real estate does not differ materially in pay structure from other practice areas at the same firm.

The ranges below are general indications for larger Dutch and international firms, and do not apply specifically to this practice area or to any particular firm. Actual pay varies by firm, region and role, and is listed where known in the individual vacancies on this site.

From partner level, the nature of compensation changes. A partner typically shares in the firm's profits rather than receiving a fixed salary, which ties pay more closely to their own client portfolio and the results of the practice group as a whole.

A day in the life

A typical day for a junior lawyer

The day often starts with reviewing due diligence findings or preparing a draft purchase agreement. On days without a client meeting, much of the time goes into drafting transaction documents and reviewing lease agreements.

A real estate lawyer often works on several transactions at once, each with its own timeline toward the notarial transfer. That requires strict planning, since missing a deadline can disrupt an entire transaction schedule.

On a day with a negotiation or closing, the pace shifts entirely. The lawyer prepares the position, coordinates with the notary, and supports the client through to signing.

Juniors

What trainee lawyers do

Junior lawyers carry much of the research and drafting work a property transaction rests on, under the supervision of a supervising partner.

  • Carrying out due diligence
  • Drafting draft purchase agreements
  • Searching Land Registry records
  • Reviewing lease agreements
  • Drafting draft advice
  • Tracking transaction deadlines
  • Sitting in on client meetings
  • Correspondence with the notary

Much of this work goes back and forth several times between trainee and supervisor before a document is signed. That repetition is not inefficiency, it is how a junior lawyer learns which risks in a property transaction really matter.

Compare

Real Estate versus other practice areas

These practice areas often overlap in the same matters, which is why students sometimes mix them up. The distinction mainly lies in which part of the transaction or dispute is at the centre.

Real Estate vs Corporate/M&A

Corporate/M&A guides the acquisition of a company. Real Estate specifically guides the property transaction itself, whether standalone or part of a larger deal.

Real Estate vs Commercial

Commercial drafts general trading contracts. Real Estate specifically drafts purchase and lease agreements for property.

Real Estate vs Tax

Tax structures the tax consequences of a property transaction, such as transfer tax. Real Estate structures the transaction itself.

Real Estate vs Dispute Resolution

Dispute Resolution conducts the proceeding once a property dispute escalates to court. Real Estate focuses on the underlying transaction or leasing relationship.

Real Estate vs Administrative Law

Administrative Law handles disputes with the government over permits. Real Estate advises more broadly on the property transaction towards its own client.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is real estate law?

Real estate law is the practice area centred on the purchase, sale, lease, financing and development of property.

What is the difference between leasing residential and commercial space?

Leasing residential space carries extensive tenant protection, for example around rent and termination. Leasing commercial space follows its own, partly different rules, giving parties more freedom to agree terms themselves.

What does a real estate lawyer do day to day?

Real estate lawyers draft purchase and lease agreements, carry out due diligence, advise on financing and area development, and handle property disputes.

What is a letter of intent in a property transaction?

A letter of intent sets out the key agreements and process before parties enter into the final purchase agreement, often including agreements on exclusivity and the due diligence period.

What does the Environment and Planning Act govern for property projects?

The Environment and Planning Act bundles the rules for the physical environment, including construction, zoning plans and environmental permits relevant to area development.

What is the Municipal Pre-emption Rights Act?

This act gives municipalities, under certain conditions, the right to buy a designated plot first before the owner may sell it to another party.

What skills do you need for real estate?

Precise writing, negotiation skills, commercial awareness, attention to detail, and the ability to work under the pressure of transaction deadlines.

How do I get into this field as a student?

Take electives in property law and tenancy law, do an internship at a firm or property investor, and apply to structured traineeships that offer a rotation in this field.
